# Chapter Section: Evaluating Data Analyst Candidates — The Team Lead’s Perspective

As a Data Team Lead at UrbanStyle, technical proficiency in SQL, Python, or BI tools is only the baseline. What truly separates a solid candidate from an exceptional hire is how effectively they work within our cross-functional environment. When evaluating Data Analyst (DA) candidates, I look at four core behavioral pillars: **Independence**, **Communication**, **Collaboration**, and **Growth Potential**.

## 1. Core Evaluation Pillars & Criteria

### 🧠 Independence: Autonomous Problem Solving
A strong candidate doesn't just wait for explicit instructions—they own the problem end-to-end.
* **What I look for:** The ability to unblock oneself. When facing messy data or ambiguous business questions, can the candidate form hypotheses, investigate data schemas independently, and validate their findings before escalating?
* **UrbanStyle Context:** If our e-commerce checkout funnel data drops unexpectedly, an independent analyst doesn't just report "data is missing." They check pipeline logs, cross-reference transaction IDs across payment gateways, and bring a diagnosed issue along with 2–3 possible root causes.

### 🗣️ Communication: Translating Complexity into Business Clarity
Data is useless if stakeholders cannot understand or act on it.
* **What I look for:** Clear, jargon-free explanations tailored to the audience. I evaluate whether candidates lead with the **"so what?"** (business impact and recommendations) rather than getting bogged down in technical methodology.
* **UrbanStyle Context:** When presenting a customer churn analysis to the Marketing Team, the analyst doesn't spend 15 minutes explaining logistic regression coefficients; instead, they explain: *"Customers who don't make a second purchase within 30 days are 60% more likely to churn—here are three targeted re-engagement campaigns we recommend."*

### 🤝 Collaboration: Active Contribution to Team & Business Goals
Data analysis is a team sport at UrbanStyle, involving product managers, fashion buyers, and software engineers.
* **What I look for:** Willingness to share knowledge, document workflows, seek peer feedback early, and proactively align with cross-functional stakeholders.
* **UrbanStyle Context:** A collaborative analyst documents their dbt models and SQL queries in our shared repository, seeks peer review before pushing major pipeline changes, and works closely with the inventory team to refine metric definitions (e.g., standardizing how "Return Rate" is calculated across departments).

### 📈 Growth Potential: Receptivity & Self-Driven Learning
Tools and frameworks change rapidly; curiosity and adaptability are non-negotiable.
* **What I look for:** A growth mindset. How does the candidate handle constructive feedback? How do they reflect on past technical or project failures, and what steps do they take to upskill?
* **UrbanStyle Context:** An analyst who notices our manual reporting processes for weekly campaign performance takes the initiative to learn Airflow or automated scheduling to streamline the team's workflow.

## 2. Green Flags & Red Flags Summary

| Flag Category | Behavioral Indicator | Real-World UrbanStyle Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🟢 **Green Flag 1** | **Structured Self-Debugging & Problem Solving** | Shows a clear framework for troubleshooting data discrepancies before asking for help. |
| 🟢 **Green Flag 2** | **Business-First Communication** | Starts presentations with key takeaways and actionable business decisions, saving technical details for the appendix. |
| 🟢 **Green Flag 3** | **Proactive Knowledge Sharing & Documentation** | Maintains clean repositories, clear documentation, and actively mentors peers or shares tool tips. |
| 🔴 **Red Flag 1** | **"Black Box" / Isolated Execution** | Works in isolation without checking in, resulting in outputs that miss the business objective or conflict with team standards. |
| 🔴 **Red Flag 2** | **Defensiveness Toward Feedback** | Argues against technical feedback or metric definitions rather than seeking to understand the stakeholder's perspective. |
